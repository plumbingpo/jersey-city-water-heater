A gas water heater is a fairly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. Most failures trace back to one of those parts. In Jersey City, NJ, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Jersey City Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Jersey City call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

When to Call for Gas Water Heater Services

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • Rust-colored hot water or rust streaks on the tank
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)

Consider the risks first. Fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust laced with carbon monoxide come with every gas water heater. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
