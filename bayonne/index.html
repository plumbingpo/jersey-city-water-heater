Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Bayonne

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
